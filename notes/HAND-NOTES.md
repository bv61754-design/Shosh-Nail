# The hand: what the owner is complaining about, and what the references show

He said the hand in the site is "unrealistic and very cartoonish". He is right.
`current-hands-too-cartoonish.jpg` is his screenshot of what the site renders today.

## What real hands in those photos have that ours does not

1. **Proportion.** Real fingers are LONG and slim relative to the palm; ours are short and thick,
   which is most of the "cartoon" read. In the references the middle finger's visible length is
   clearly greater than the width of the palm. Ours is roughly equal.
2. **Taper along the whole finger**, not just at the tip: each finger narrows steadily from knuckle
   to nail bed, and narrows again slightly at each joint before widening a touch at the pad.
3. **The fingers are not parallel tubes.** They splay from the knuckles and each one curves very
   slightly; adjacent fingers touch along part of their length and separate toward the tips.
4. **Soft, low-contrast skin.** The shading in the photos is gentle and mostly at the silhouette
   edges — there is far less internal detail than an illustrator wants to draw. Our knuckle and
   tendon detail is louder than reality even now that it is warm.
5. **The nail occupies most of the fingertip width** and its side walls come very close to the
   skin folds; the pad of the finger barely shows past the nail on a long press-on.
6. **The fingertip is not a hemisphere.** It is slightly flattened where the nail sits, and the flesh
   under a long nail is visible as a thin lighter strip.
7. **Nothing is symmetric.** Real fingers differ subtly in length, width and angle.

## The honest limit

A vector illustration will not become a photograph. Pushing the drawing gets us to "a good
illustration of a hand"; it will not pass for a photo. If genuinely photographic hands are the goal,
the only real path is a PHOTO of a real hand owned by the shop, with the nail plates rendered on
top — see the separate proposal.
